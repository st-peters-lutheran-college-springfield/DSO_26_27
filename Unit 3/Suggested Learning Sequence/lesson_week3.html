Week 3: Progress Check, the ESP32-S3 Kit & Success Criteria

Lessons 8–11 · Tuesday 20 October 2026 – Friday 23 October 2026

Four 60-minute lessonsProgress check: Tuesday 20 OctoberHands-on kit lesson: Wednesday 21 October
Start here: welcome back! This week starts with a check of your journal from the relief-cover fortnight, then catches up on the useability principles and the hands-on kit work, before turning your analysis into requirements and success criteria.

This Week's Lessons

Lesson 8 — Tuesday 20 October 2026
Week 3 · Day 7, Period 3 (60 min) · Objective(s) 1, 3
Learning intentionCheck your progress from the relief-cover fortnight and learn the five QCAA useability principles.
Direct teaching (15–20 min)Resource 2: Useability Principles & Heuristic Evaluation. In Lesson 4 you met general usability heuristics; this resource introduces the five useability principles QCAA assesses, and how to run a heuristic review with them.
What to do
  • Progress check: your teacher will check your OneNote journal for every Weeks 1–2 entry: IA1 task summary, data fields, system sketch, chosen heuristics, persona, display sketches, and the fields needed for on-time/delayed/alert status.
  • If anything is missing, finish it first.
  • Then copy the heuristic review table from Resource 2 into your journal and complete it for brisbus.com: evidence and a judgement for each of the five principles.
  • Record any feedback from your teacher straight away.
Resources
  • OneNote project journal
  • Resource 2: Useability Principles & Heuristic Evaluation
  • brisbus.com
Add to your OneNote journalA dated note of your progress-check feedback and your completed brisbus.com heuristic review table.

Lesson 9 — Wednesday 21 October 2026
Week 3 · Day 8, Period 5 (60 min) · Objective(s) 1
Learning intentionRecognise and describe the components of an intelligent system using the real ESP32-S3 kit.
Direct teaching (15–20 min)Resource 1: Innovation, Emerging Technologies & Intelligent Systems Components. Learn what innovation means in this task and the seven components of an intelligent system, then complete the check for understanding.
What to do
  • Unbox and assemble the Keyestudio ESP32-S3 kit on a breadboard, following your teacher's instructions.
  • Identify each hardware component: the board, the 1.54″ display, the MAX98357A speaker, and the INMP441 microphone.
  • Using the component table in Resource 1, classify each part (actuator, user-interface component, network hardware, and so on) and describe what it would do in your Next Bus device.
  • Revisit your Lesson 6 display sketches: does seeing the real screen size change anything?
  • Pack the kit away as instructed at the end of the lesson.
Resources
  • Keyestudio ESP32-S3 kit (KS-KS5027)
  • Resource 1: Innovation, Emerging Technologies & Intelligent Systems Components
Add to your OneNote journalA photo of your assembled kit, your component classification, and your check-for-understanding answers.

Lesson 10 — Thursday 22 October 2026
Week 3 · Day 9, Period 2 (60 min) · Objective(s) 4
Learning intentionDetermine user-experience requirements from the passenger's point of view, and programming requirements from the developer's.
What to do
  • Open your persona from Lesson 5 and your list of data fields from Lesson 7.
  • Build a table that maps each data field to the display or spoken state it supports: on time, delayed, or service alert. Mark any field your device doesn't need.
  • Draft your user-experience requirements from your persona's point of view (for example, ‘The passenger can tell whether their bus is late without reading small text’).
  • Draft your programming requirements from the developer's point of view: what the device must read, store, calculate, and decide.
Resources
  • Persona (Week 2)
  • Requirements template (OneNote Class Notebook)
Add to your OneNote journalYour data-field-to-state mapping table, plus your draft user-experience and programming requirements.

Lesson 11 — Friday 23 October 2026
Week 3 · Day 10, Period 4 (60 min) · Objective(s) 4
Learning intentionDetermine measurable success criteria, including the personal, social, and economic impacts of your device.
Direct teaching (15–20 min)Resource 4: Personal, Social & Economic Impact Evaluation. Work through the worked example, then complete the check for understanding for your own device.
What to do
  • Success-criteria workshop: draft four to five measurable success criteria for your device. Strong examples: ‘readable at 1.5 m’, ‘understandable without training’, ‘distinguishes on time from delayed without relying on colour alone’.
  • Make sure your criteria cover the personal, social, and economic impacts of the solution, each naming a specific stakeholder (not just ‘users’).
  • Swap with a partner: could each criterion be clearly tested as met or not met? Rewrite any that couldn't.
Resources
  • Success criteria template (OneNote Class Notebook)
  • Resource 4: Personal, Social & Economic Impact Evaluation
Add to your OneNote journalYour four to five draft success criteria and your personal, social, and economic impact sentences.

Log Your Evidence

At the end of each lesson this week, add a timestamped entry to your OneNote project journal (see the Evidence Journal Guide). Your teacher checks your journal from Weeks 1–2 in Lesson 8.
