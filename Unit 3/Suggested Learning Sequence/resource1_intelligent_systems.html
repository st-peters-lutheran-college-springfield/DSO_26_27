Resource 1: Innovation, Emerging Technologies & Intelligent Systems Components

IA1 direct-teaching resource · Next Bus, Made Simple · Used in Lessons 9 and 12

15–20 minute segmentConcept first, then apply to your device
Start here: learn the concept in general terms first, then use the Next Bus examples to connect it to your own device.
Syllabus (Digital Solutions 2025 v1.4): “Explore and analyse the meaning and importance of innovation…”; “Explore the impact of emerging technologies on innovation, e.g. IoT, intelligent systems, embedded sensing”; “Recognise and describe components of a digital solution appropriate to the technology context selected” (Topic 1).

1. What Counts as Innovation Here?

Innovation in Digital Solutions doesn't mean inventing new hardware. It means applying an existing technology (open transit data, a small embedded device) to solve a real, previously unsolved need for a specific group of people.

The personal, business, and social opportunity in this task is specific: older Queenslanders with limited smartphone use currently have no non-screen way to check real-time transit information.

2. The Intelligent Systems Technology Context

Unit 3 offers four technology contexts: web applications, mobile applications, interactive media, and intelligent systems. IA1 uses Intelligent Systems, which the syllabus defines by a specific set of components — different from a website or app.

ComponentIn the Next Bus device
SensorsNone required for this design. The ‘sensor’ is the incoming GTFS-realtime data feed itself, not a physical sensor on the device.
ActuatorsThe 1.54″ display and MAX98357A speaker: the components that act on incoming data to produce an output a person can perceive.
User-interface componentsDisplay screen, speaker, and any physical buttons used for input (for example, selecting a stop or route).
Analogue/digital input-output data streamsThe GTFS-realtime feed arriving over Wi-Fi as digital data; the audio/visual output produced for the user.
Administrative interface componentsAny setup step a carer or user performs once, for example selecting which bus stop or route the device should track.
Network hardware and protocolsThe ESP32-S3's Wi-Fi module, connecting to Translink's GTFS-realtime feed over HTTP(S).
Internal data structuresThe way the device stores the fields it needs after reading the feed (for example, route, scheduled time, predicted time, delay, and status).

3. The Real Data Source

  • Translink publishes a live, public GTFS-realtime feed for South East Queensland. No authentication or account is required.
  • It has three parts: Vehicle Positions (live vehicle location), Trip Updates (predicted arrival and departure times, delays), and Service Alerts (disruption notices).
  • The feed is encoded in Google's Protobuf format, following the GTFS-realtime v2.0 specification — an open standard used by transit agencies worldwide, not something Translink invented.
  • For IA1 you are given a sample CSV extract of this data (springfield_central_transit_snapshot.csv), so you don't need to parse the live feed yourself. Comparing the binary Protobuf feed with a flat-file CSV is a good example of different file formats and data structures suited to different purposes.

Source: Translink Open Data, GTFS Realtime (translink.com.au/about-translink/open-data/gtfs-rt), accessed September 2026.
