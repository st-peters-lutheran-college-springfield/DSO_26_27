Resource 4: Personal, Social & Economic Impact Evaluation

IA1 direct-teaching resource · Next Bus, Made Simple · Used in Lesson 11

15–20 minute segmentConcept first, then apply to your device
Start here: learn the concept in general terms first, then use the Next Bus examples to connect it to your own device.
Syllabus (Digital Solutions 2025 v1.4): “Determine possible personal, social and economic impacts” (Topic 1). The IA1 task sheet requires “success criteria that evaluate the personal, social, and economic impacts of the solution.”
QCAA does not formally define ‘personal’, ‘social’, and ‘economic’ as separate terms in the Digital Solutions syllabus. The three-way split is a widely used teaching scaffold. Use the working definitions below as reasoning support, and make sure your response also addresses the syllabus's own stakeholder scope: individuals, organisations, and governments.

1. Working Definitions

Impact typeWorking definition and prompt questions
PersonalChanges how a person does something, or affects their opinions, knowledge, or wellbeing. Ask: does this reduce anxiety or increase independence for an individual older passenger?
SocialAlters the wellbeing of the surrounding or wider community. Ask: does this change how families and carers relate to an older relative's independence? Could it affect passengers who don't have the device?
EconomicAffects an economic system at a local, national, or global level: revenue, costs, employment, or efficiency. Ask: does it reduce missed-service costs for Translink or aged-care providers, or create a cost barrier if the device isn't free?

Definitions adapted from a CSIRO-derived impact framework, as compiled in a teacher-authored Digital Solutions resource (Murtagh, damom73.github.io/digital-solutions-text-v2). Informal reference, not an official QCAA publication.

2. Worked Example (a Different Scenario)

See the method on an unrelated device first: a smart fridge that tracks expiry dates and reorders groceries automatically.

ImpactSmart fridge example
PersonalReduces the mental load of tracking what's in the fridge for someone with a busy schedule.
SocialCould reduce household food waste, but also raises questions about who in a shared household controls the reordering.
EconomicA possible subscription cost for the reordering service; possible savings from less spoiled food.

3. Applying This to Your Success Criteria

In Lesson 11, use these impacts to shape your success criteria. At least some of your criteria should be checkable statements about personal, social, or economic outcomes for a named stakeholder.

Check for Understanding

Write one sentence each — Personal, Social, Economic — for your own Next Bus device, naming a specific stakeholder each time (not just ‘users’).
