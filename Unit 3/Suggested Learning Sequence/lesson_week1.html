Week 1: Exploring the Problem & the Data

Lessons 1–3 · Wednesday 7 October 2026 – Friday 9 October 2026

Three 60-minute lessonsNo checkpoint this weekRelief cover: theory only
Start here: Mrs Baessler is away this fortnight. Work through each lesson below with your relief teacher. Each lesson has a short task and something to add to your OneNote project journal — keep it up to date, because Mrs Baessler will check your journal in Lesson 8 when she's back.
No hands-on kit work this fortnight. We'll unbox and build with the ESP32-S3 kits together once Mrs Baessler is back. These two weeks are about understanding the problem and the data.

This Week's Lessons

Lesson 1 — Wednesday 7 October 2026
Week 1 · Day 8, Period 5 (60 min) · Objective(s) 1
Learning intentionUnderstand the IA1 task and start exploring the problem.
What to do
  • As a class, read the IA1 task sheet: the Context, Task, and Conditions sections.
  • Join the class discussion: what problems might an older person with limited smartphone use have when catching a bus? There are no wrong answers here — just get ideas out.
  • On your own, write a OneNote journal entry (3–4 sentences, in your own words) explaining what the IA1 task is asking you to do.
Add to your OneNote journalA short paragraph (3–4 sentences) explaining the IA1 task in your own words.

Lesson 2 — Thursday 8 October 2026
Week 1 · Day 9, Period 2 (60 min) · Objective(s) 2
Learning intentionFind out what data the Translink real-time feed actually provides.
What to do
  • Open springfield_central_transit_snapshot.csv (shared in the class Teams channel) in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets. Read the README file first.
  • With a partner, list every data field recorded for each service — for example: stop, route, headsign, scheduled time, predicted time, delay, status, vehicle type.
  • Write a OneNote journal entry listing the data fields you found, and note which 2–3 fields you think would matter most to a passenger who's anxious about missing their bus.
Add to your OneNote journalThe list of data fields, plus which ones matter most to your target passenger and why.

Lesson 3 — Friday 9 October 2026
Week 1 · Day 10, Period 4 (60 min) · Objective(s) 2
Learning intentionLearn how to represent a system as a simple diagram.
What to do
  • Read/watch: Unit 3 Textbook, Chapter 3.1 (Exploring innovation) — the section on representing systems.
  • With a partner, sketch a simple box-and-arrow diagram on paper showing how information could flow: open transit data → a small device → a display or speaker for the passenger.
  • It doesn't need to use correct data-flow-diagram notation yet — Mrs Baessler will help you formalise this when she's back.
  • Take a photo of your sketch and add it to your OneNote journal with one sentence describing it.
Add to your OneNote journalA photo of your sketch, plus one sentence describing what it shows.

Log Your Evidence

At the end of each lesson this week, add a timestamped entry to your OneNote project journal (see the Evidence Journal Guide). Mrs Baessler checks these in Lesson 8 (Tuesday 20 October), and they feed into Checkpoint 1.
