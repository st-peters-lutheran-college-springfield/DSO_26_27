Week 2: Existing Solutions, Usability & the ESP32-S3 Kit

Lessons 4–7 · Monday 12 October 2026 – Friday 16 October 2026

Four 60-minute lessonsNo checkpoint this weekRelief cover: theory only
Start here: Mrs Baessler is away this fortnight. Work through each lesson below with your relief teacher. Each lesson has a short task and something to add to your OneNote project journal — keep it up to date, because Mrs Baessler will check your journal in Lesson 8 when she's back.
No hands-on kit work this fortnight. We'll unbox and build with the ESP32-S3 kits together once Mrs Baessler is back. These two weeks are about understanding the problem and the data.

This Week's Lessons

Lesson 4 — Monday 12 October 2026
Week 2 · Day 1, Period 4 (60 min) · Objective(s) 1, 3
Learning intentionLearn the usability principles you'll use to judge your own design.
What to do
  • Read/watch: Unit 3 Textbook, Chapter 3.4 (Evaluating a digital solution) — the Usability heuristics section.
  • As a class, test Translink's own journey planner (translink.com.au) against 2–3 of the heuristics. Where does it fall short for someone less confident with smartphones?
  • Write a OneNote journal entry: which 2 heuristics do you think matter most for your device, and why?
Add to your OneNote journalYour 2 chosen heuristics and a short explanation of why they matter most for this task.

Lesson 5 — Tuesday 13 October 2026
Week 2 · Day 2, Period 2 (60 min) · Objective(s) 3
Learning intentionLook at an existing solution and start defining who you're designing for.
What to do
  • As a class, explore brisbus.com (Brisbane Buses — Live Map) — a real existing solution built on the same open transit data your device will use.
  • Discuss: what does it do well? Where would it be hard to use for an older, less digitally confident, or vision-impaired passenger?
  • On your own, write a short persona for the passenger your device is designed for: a name, age, situation, and 1–2 specific difficulties they have. This must be a made-up/composite character — do not base it on or name a real person, including family members.
  • Add your persona to your OneNote journal (4–6 sentences).
Add to your OneNote journalYour persona (4–6 sentences: made-up name, age, situation, specific difficulties).

Lesson 6 — Thursday 15 October 2026
Week 2 · Day 4, Period 4 (60 min) · Objective(s) 1
Learning intentionExplore what a small screen, speaker and microphone can (and can't) communicate.
What to do
  • Your relief teacher will show you photos and the spec sheet for the Keyestudio ESP32-S3 kit (the kits stay boxed this lesson — we'll build with them properly once Mrs Baessler is back).
  • As a class, discuss: given a small screen, a speaker, and a microphone, what could this device show or say to a passenger? What can't it do (there's no touchscreen, for example)?
  • On your own, sketch 1–2 rough ideas for what the display might show for ‘on time’ vs ‘delayed’.
  • Take a photo of your sketch and add it to your OneNote journal.
Add to your OneNote journalA photo of your ‘on time’ / ‘delayed’ display sketches.

Lesson 7 — Friday 16 October 2026
Week 2 · Day 5, Period 5 (60 min) · Objective(s) 3, 4
Learning intentionPull together the data understanding from earlier in the fortnight.
What to do
  • Look back at springfield_central_transit_snapshot.csv from Lesson 2.
  • On your own, identify exactly which data fields you'd need to work out ‘on time’ vs ‘delayed’ vs ‘service alert’ for your device.
  • Write this up in your OneNote journal.
  • Check your OneNote journal has an entry for every lesson this fortnight (persona, existing-solutions notes, data fields, sketches) — Mrs Baessler will check these in Lesson 8.
Add to your OneNote journalThe specific data fields needed to determine on-time/delayed/service-alert status.

Log Your Evidence

At the end of each lesson this week, add a timestamped entry to your OneNote project journal (see the Evidence Journal Guide). Mrs Baessler checks these in Lesson 8 (Tuesday 20 October), and they feed into Checkpoint 1.
